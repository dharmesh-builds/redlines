## Findings detail

1. **Auto-renewal and cancellation friction.** FTC press release: "in 2024 the Commission received nearly 70 consumer complaints per day on average, up from 42 per day in 2021," and "thousands of complaints about negative option and recurring subscription practices each year." Practices targeted: failing to clearly disclose terms before collecting billing information, charging without informed consent, and no simple way to cancel. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring. The Click-to-Cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 (procedural: missing preliminary regulatory analysis). The FTC issued a new ANPRM in March 2026 and still enforces under ROSCA, per law-firm summaries: https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses. Dollar figures ($1.3B across 14 cases; Amazon $1B plus $1.5B) came from a search summary of advocacy and press sources. See https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/the-cost-of-trapping-subscribers/ and verify before use.

2. **Freelancer nonpayment and late payment.** Freelancers Union NY survey: "sixty-two percent of freelance workers based in New York had lost wages at least once"; "91% of survey respondents reported experiencing late or overdue wages at least once in their career"; "54% reporting experiencing delays of 3 months or longer"; "Fifty-three percent reported losing as much as $10,000"; "39% of respondents said non or overdue payment affected their ability to pay bills or rent." The page gave no median amount owed. The 2015 national Freelancers Union survey (5,358 respondents) reported about half had trouble getting paid and an average longest wait of 98 days, per a search summary. Source: https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2022/05/12/over-60-of-ny-freelancers-report-not-being-paid-for-work-performed.

3. **Rental late fees.** CFPB: "the average late fee is $85, up significantly from September 2021"; "Just under 60% of those who incur any late fees experience two or more"; "More than 20% of renters with at least one late fee have five or more late fees in the last twelve months"; outstanding rental balance rose from $2,000 (Sep 2021) to $3,200 (Nov 2024). The share of renters paying late fees fell from 23% (Feb 2023) to slightly under 14% (Nov 2024). Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-report-finds-continued-challenges-for-households-that-rent. The CFPB page did not give complaint counts or mention lease terms.

4. **Security deposits and lease breaks.** From a search summary of Tenants Together's *No Deterrent* report (California): 60% of surveyed members experienced unfair withholding; tenants prevailed in over 70% of cases reaching judgment, but only 3.5% of tenant-filed deposit cases resulted in a landlord penalty; an estimated $5B in deposits held by CA landlords. Rent.com 2013 survey (via AOL/Yahoo): 26% of renters denied deposits, early move-out the top reason (44% of 18-24 year-olds cited lease breaking). I could not open the original report (node/9253 returned 404). Sources: https://www.tenantstogether.org/node/8539 ; https://www.aol.com/news/2013-01-29-security-deposit-refund.html. Both are dated (2012-13) and the first is California-only.

5. **Non-compete.** FTC: "estimated 30 million workers," "nearly one in five Americans"; "additional $524 per year" for the average worker; health-care costs lower "by up to $194 billion over the next decade." The page states the rule "is not in effect and it is not enforceable" after the August 2024 stay. These are FTC projections of the benefit of a ban, not measured losses. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes.

6. **Arbitration and class-action waivers.** CFPB 2015 study, per Goodwin: "around 90 percent of arbitration clauses in consumer contracts include a prohibition on class arbitration"; "75 percent of credit card consumers did not know whether their contracts had arbitration clauses"; "about 32 million individuals are eligible for relief through class actions every year" and "about $220 million a year" is paid out; consumers file "about 1,200 per year" individual lawsuits and "about 600 per year" arbitrations. Source: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2015/03/cfpb-finds-arbitration-agreements-limit-consumer-redress. The CFPB's arbitration rule was later nullified by Congress. That is my background knowledge, not from this research. Industry critics disputed the study's methodology (search summary).

7. **Limitation of liability.** Search summary of construction and engineering case-law blogs: courts enforced a cap at fees paid ($80,514); limited recovery to $550,000 on a $9.5M jury verdict; capped potential damages at $75,000 (Column Form Tech v. Caraustar, Del. Super. 2014). Courts "rarely" find such clauses unconscionable between businesses. Sources: https://rtspecialty.com/ecp/limitation-of-liability-clause-enforced/ ; https://www.constructionrisk.com/limitation-of-liability-clause/. These are snippet-level and construction and engineering only; I did not find a SaaS or freelance example. Cap wording matters: one court read "fees paid" as excluding unpaid fees.

8. **Most-negotiated terms (WorldCC).** Per search summary of the 2024 report: limitation of liability, price/charges and indemnification hold the top rankings; for SMEs the top three are limitation of liability, indemnification, price/charges. Overall top 10 also includes termination, payment, scope, warranty, IP, delivery, liquidated damages. The 2022 report found only 4 of the top 10 most negotiated terms were also in the top 10 most important. I could not read the PDF (the fetch returned a members-only index), so rankings are unverified against the source. This measures negotiation effort among contracting professionals, not harm. URL: https://www.worldcc.com/Portals/IACCM/Reports/Most-Negotiated-Terms-2024.pdf.

9. **People do not read the terms.** Bakos, Marotta-Wurgler and Trossen: of about 48,000 visitors to 90 software vendor sites, only one or two per 1,000 accessed the license agreement. Obar and Oeldorf-Hirsch: 98% of participants agreed to a clause giving up a firstborn child; 74% skipped the privacy policy. Source: https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/jls/vol43/iss1/2 and https://www.npr.org/2016/08/23/491024846/do-you-read-terms-of-service-contracts-not-many-do-research-shows (search summary only). This supports the premise that there is an unread-contract problem. It does not show which clauses cause harm.

## Notes on ranking method

- **Measured:** complaint rates for auto-renewal (FTC); incidence and dollar loss for freelancer nonpayment (Freelancers Union); late fee incidence and amounts (CFPB); class-waiver prevalence (CFPB 2015).
- **Judgment (mine):** the ordering itself. The sources use different populations (all US consumers, NY freelancers, CA tenants, B2B negotiators), different years (2012 to 2026) and different metrics, so no common frequency or dollar scale exists. I put auto-renewal first because it has the only recent, national, rising complaint series plus billion-dollar enforcement outcomes. I put payment terms second because incidence is very high (62% to 91%) and loss is concrete, though NY-only.
- **Weakest rows:** ranks 4, 7 and 8 rest on search snippets. Rank 5 is a projection of benefit, not a measured loss. Ranks 7-8 measure negotiation attention, not harm.
- B2B clauses (caps, indemnity) rank lower here only because consumer and freelancer sources dominated; severity per incident can be far higher (the $9.5M verdict reduced to $550K) but I found no frequency data.

## Evidence that cuts against the hypothesis

- **Harm is often enforcement and bargaining power, not comprehension.** Freelancers Union found "less than one percent of respondents used" the legal system and 79% tried their own demands. Clause-reading does not fix a client who will not pay. The proposed NY remedy is a statute (written contract at $250+, payment within 30 days, double damages), not better reading.
- **Auto-renewal harm is mostly a cancellation-flow problem.** The FTC's complaints are about "endless hoops" to cancel and charges without consent. Those are UI and behavior problems that a contract summary does not address, and consumers often never see a document.
- **Deposit and late-fee harm is mostly enforcement.** Tenants won over 70% of deposit cases that reached judgment but landlords were penalized in only 3.5% (snippet-level). That is a remedy gap, not a reading gap. Late fees are often visible in the lease, but tenants have no leverage to change them.
- **Knowing a cap exists does not remove it.** Courts enforce caps between businesses and rarely find them unconscionable, so a flag helps only if the reader has leverage to negotiate.
- **Most-negotiated is not most-important.** WorldCC found only 4 of the top 10 most negotiated terms are also in the top 10 most important (search summary). Negotiation volume among contract professionals may not match reader harm.
- **Almost nobody reads contracts**, so the real buyer moment may be before signing at all. That is an adoption risk, since a tool the user must open first competes with the 0.1% base rate.
- **Regulatory flux.** The FTC non-compete rule is not in effect, and Click-to-Cancel was vacated. Both signal policy risk and that some harm may be addressed outside contracts.

## What I could not find

- Any measured frequency or dollar-loss data for **IP assignment** (broad or pre-existing IP). Only practitioner blog advice and sample clause language.
- Any data on **unilateral change / unilateral termination** clauses specifically. No source quantified harm.
- Complaint-count breakdowns by clause type from CFPB or state AGs. The CFPB rental pages gave no complaint counts (a search summary mentioned 1,700+ rental debt complaints, unverified).
- **Kill fees, net-60/90 terms and fee escalators.** No sourced data. The Freelancers Union page did not mention kill fees.
- **Indemnity** dollar outcomes or frequency, beyond WorldCC negotiation rank.
- The WorldCC 2024 report itself (members-only; fetch returned an index page). The NCLC 2023 rental junk-fee comment (PDF unreadable by the fetcher; it contains a survey of 95 legal-services attorneys in 26 states per a search summary). The Tenants Together report original (404).
- Reddit and forum complaint threads. I did not search them within the cap, so the anecdotal and recurring-complaint angle is unrepresented.
- Lease-break and early-termination fee data with figures. Only that NCLC criticizes termination fees that ignore re-rental.
- Any quantification of how often a plain-English summary or counteroffer would have changed an outcome. All evidence here is about harm, not about the effectiveness of the product.
